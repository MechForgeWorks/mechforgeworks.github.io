Engineer. Physical systems, manufacturing, and the computational layer behind them.

I work on how vehicles get made - whether a design can actually be built, what the automated assembly around it has to do, and what needs to change before anyone commits to tooling. Eight years across automotive and off-highway manufacturers.

What I do

Manufacturing feasibility
Working out whether a design can be built, and what building it will cost, while the design can still change cheaply. Layout, process concepts, and the constraints that come back from the production line.
Automated assembly
Vehicle body assembly is largely robotic. I work on the equipment and process strategy around it - what the cells need to do, how parts are located and joined, and where a process is likely to fail before it does.
Engineering data and systems
Working day to day in manufacturing systems - NX, Teamcenter, 3DX. Reviewing design and process data so problems surface on a screen instead of on a production line. Catching things early is almost the entire value of the job.

Where I'm heading

The problems I find most interesting here are increasingly computational - simulation, machine learning applied to process and quality data, and closing the loop between what a model predicts and what a line actually does. My experience is on the physical side of that boundary. I'm deliberately building the other side: learning Python, getting properly fluent with engineering data, and working out where machine learning earns its place in manufacturing. A direction I'm moving in, rather than one I've arrived at.
